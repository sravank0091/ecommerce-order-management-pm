# Lessons Learned and Retrospective

## 1. Project Overview

**Project:** E-commerce Order Management System  
**Document:** Lessons Learned and Project Retrospective  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Illustrative  
**Project Type:** Fictional IT Project Management Portfolio Case Study

## 2. Purpose

This document demonstrates how a project manager can capture lessons, identify process improvements, and apply learning to future technology delivery initiatives.

All observations below are hypothetical examples, not actual findings from an employer project.

## 3. Sample Lessons Learned

| ID | Project Area | Illustrative Observation | Recommended Improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| LL-01 | Requirements | Late requirement changes may affect sprint commitments and dependencies. | Establish a requirements baseline and formal change-control process. |
| LL-02 | Stakeholder Communication | Different teams may have different expectations about project status. | Use a consistent status report and stakeholder communication cadence. |
| LL-03 | Integration Testing | Delayed availability of interfaces may affect SIT execution. | Track integration dependencies and confirm readiness before testing begins. |
| LL-04 | Risk Management | Risks may become issues when ownership and actions are unclear. | Assign every significant risk an owner, mitigation action, and review date. |
| LL-05 | Release Readiness | Incomplete support preparation may complicate go-live. | Include support readiness and rollback validation in release approval criteria. |
| LL-06 | Reporting | Manual status consolidation may consume project team time. | Standardize reporting templates and automate appropriate metrics where feasible. |

## 4. What Went Well

The following are examples of practices that could support successful delivery:

- Defined project scope and measurable objectives.
- Regular stakeholder communication and escalation.
- Clear ownership of deliverables and dependencies.
- Structured SIT and UAT planning.
- Documented production readiness and rollback criteria.

These are illustrative practices, not verified outcomes of an executed project.

## 5. Improvement Opportunities

| Opportunity | Proposed Action | Responsible Role |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements traceability | Maintain links between requirements, test cases, and defects. | Business Analyst / QA Lead |
| Dependency management | Review cross-team dependencies during weekly governance meetings. | Project Manager |
| Defect resolution | Establish severity-based escalation and ownership. | QA Lead / Technical Lead |
| Release communication | Publish a go-live communication and escalation plan. | Project Manager |
| Post-launch support | Conduct daily hypercare reviews and track open incidents. | Project Manager / Support Lead |

## 6. Action Plan for Future Projects

| Action | Priority | Success Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce a standardized RAID review | High | Risks and issues have owners and review dates |
| Establish requirements traceability | High | Agreed requirements map to acceptance tests |
| Validate integration readiness earlier | High | Dependencies are reviewed before SIT |
| Standardize stakeholder reporting | Medium | Stakeholders receive consistent project updates |
| Conduct a formal retrospective | Medium | Improvement actions are documented and assigned |

## 7. Retrospective Questions

At project closure, the team should discuss:

1. Were project objectives and acceptance criteria achieved?
2. Which dependencies or risks had the greatest impact?
3. Were stakeholders informed at the right time?
4. What should be continued, stopped, or changed?
5. Who owns each improvement action, and when will it be reviewed?

## 8. Final Notes

Lessons should be reviewed with relevant stakeholders, validated against actual project evidence, and converted into actionable improvements.
